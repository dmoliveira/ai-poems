# Rights, sources and translation policy

Verso cites the source and an explicit rights note on each poem and translation. These notes are editorial guidance, **not legal advice**; public-domain terms and moral rights differ by jurisdiction. Do not assume that a public-domain original makes a later translation, scholarly edition or transcription free to reuse.

## Launch sources

- Luís de Camões, *Amor he hum fogo*: [Portuguese Wikisource](https://pt.wikisource.org/wiki/Amor_he_hum_fogo_que_arde_sem_se_ver), historical spelling from the linked edition.
- Victor Hugo, *Demain, dès l'aube*: [French Wikisource](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Les_Contemplations/%C2%AB_Demain,_d%C3%A8s_l%E2%80%99aube,_%C3%A0_l%E2%80%99heure_o%C3%B9_blanchit_la_campagne_%C2%BB).
- Giacomo Leopardi, *L'Infinito*: [Italian Wikisource](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Versi_del_conte_Giacomo_Leopardi/L%27Infinito), 1826 edition. The poem [first appeared in *Nuovo Ricoglitore* in 1825](https://www.internetculturale.it/directories/ViaggiNelTesto/leopardi/eng/b13.html).
- Leopardi's [*La ricordanza*](https://it.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Versi_del_conte_Giacomo_Leopardi/La_ricordanza&oldid=3768364) and [*La sera del giorno festivo*](https://it.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=Versi_del_conte_Giacomo_Leopardi/La_sera_del_giorno_festivo&oldid=3768365) follow the 1826 *Versi* edition, preserving its spellings and titles. These transcriptions are credited to Wikisource contributors under CC BY-SA 4.0; consult the linked revision histories for contributors. The early *La ricordanza* differs from the later *Alla luna*.
- Frederick Townsend's English versions of *The Infinite* and *To the Moon* are separately transcribed and credited from [*The Poems of Giacomo Leopardi* (New York, 1887), Gutenberg 19315](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/19315). These translations follow later textual versions, not the exact 1826 originals; *To the Moon* includes lines absent from the earlier text. Gutenberg identifies the edition as public domain in the United States; rights elsewhere may differ.
- Li Bai, *Quiet Night Thought*: [Chinese Wikisource](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E9%9D%9C%E5%A4%9C%E6%80%9D), *Three Hundred Tang Poems* variant. The seven AI-assisted reading aids are new, unreviewed interpretations, not historical translations; verify them before relying on them.
- Matsuo Bashō, *The Old Pond*: [Japanese Wikisource, 1903 collection](https://ja.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%8A%AD%E8%95%89%E4%BF%B3%E5%8F%A5%E5%85%A8%E9%9B%86); Lafcadio Hearn's English rendering appears in [*Exotics and Retrospectives*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/42735/42735-h/42735-h.htm), first published in 1898.
- Bashō's *Summer Grass* and *The Mogami River* follow the main readings of [*Oku no Hosomichi*, Japanese Wikisource revision 247172](https://ja.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=%E3%81%8A%E3%81%8F%E3%81%AE%E3%81%BB%E3%81%9D%E9%81%93&oldid=247172), which identifies Miyake's 1911 edition as its source. Compare the [edition's scanned p. 51](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:NDL888916_%E6%96%B0%E9%87%88%E5%A5%A5%E3%81%AE%E7%B4%B0%E9%81%93.pdf?page=31) and [p. 60](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:NDL888916_%E6%96%B0%E9%87%88%E5%A5%A5%E3%81%AE%E7%B4%B0%E9%81%93.pdf?page=36). The inline variant notes are omitted from the verse, but acknowledged in context. Wikisource credits its text contributors under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/); its [source notes](https://ja.wikisource.org/wiki/%E3%83%88%E3%83%BC%E3%82%AF:%E3%81%8A%E3%81%8F%E3%81%AE%E3%81%BB%E3%81%9D%E9%81%93) name CES1596 as the transcriber; consult the linked revision history for contributors. [Iwate University](https://chs.iwate-u.ac.jp/heritage/html/literature-sub-natsukusa.html) and [Doshisha Women's College](https://www.dwc.doshisha.ac.jp/research/faculty_column/12912) corroborate the attribution and contexts, including an earlier variant of the river verse.
- Yosa Buson's *Rapeseed Flowers* and *The Spring Sea* are transcribed from [Ōno Shachiku, *Yosa Buson* (1897), pp. 132–133, scan 75](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/875927/1/75). In the spring-sea verse, the vertical repetition mark after のたり is expanded to のたり; otherwise the edition's wording is retained. The [National Diet Library's IIIF manifest](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/api/iiif/875927/manifest.json) marks the scan PDM; the 1897 edition is public domain in the United States. [Yoshikai's account](https://yaenk.sakura.ne.jp/archives/kotenkouza/3165) and a [Japanese government article](https://www.gov-online.go.jp/eng/publicity/book/hlj/html/202207/202207_12_jp.html) independently corroborate the verses' attribution; they are cited as context, not as permission to reproduce their modern text. A [library reference](https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/detail?page=ref_view&id=1000162094) describes competing evidence for the spring-sea location, which is not asserted here. Buson's death year is rendered 1783 or 1784 in different sources and calendars; the [Kyoto National Museum](https://knmdb.kyohaku.go.jp/eng/20928.html) gives the traditional date and 1784. The four English display titles are editorial labels, not full translations; no translations are supplied for these additions.
- *Let the Days Do as They Will*: traditionally attributed to Al-Shāfiʿī. The [Arabic Wikisource transcription, revision 456958](https://ar.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=%D8%AF%D8%B9_%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%85_%D8%AA%D9%81%D8%B9%D9%84_%D9%85%D8%A7_%D8%AA%D8%B4%D8%A7%D8%A1&oldid=456958) does **not** identify its source edition. It is credited under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/); its attribution and textual history need further research. Consult the linked revision history for contributors and check the applicable terms before reuse.

Wikisource pages may include transcriptions and contributions under their stated Creative Commons terms; follow each linked page and revision history for attribution and license details. The MIT license in this repository applies to application code, not automatically to third-party content. Images and fonts have separate terms; the fonts' SIL OFL texts are in `assets/fonts/`.

## Expanded historical collections

- [*Three Hundred Tang Poems*, Gutenberg 52323](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/52323): 319 selections from the numbered Chinese transcription; no. 233 is omitted because Li Bai's *Quiet Night Thought* already has a stable page. The eBook does not identify a particular print edition. Names follow the source, with readable romanizations; the source's 劉春虛 is associated with [Liu Shenxu, 劉眘虛 / 劉慎虛](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E9%97%95%E9%A1%8C_(%E5%8A%89%E7%9C%98%E8%99%9B)). 西鄙人 is an anonymous designation, not an identified person. Dates and precise creation places are not inferred.
- [*Las cien mejores poesías de la lengua castellana*, Gutenberg 65880](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/65880): the 1910 third edition selected by Marcelino Menéndez y Pelayo. It includes traditional romances and Spanish-language poets from outside Spain; the anthology's own author headings are recorded, including anonymous attributions. Section and speaker headings and historical spelling are retained.
- [*The Book of American Negro Poetry*, Gutenberg 11986](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11986): James Weldon Johnson's 1922 collection, with its historical spellings and dialect retained. Some texts reflect the racism and violence of their period. Explicitly marked excerpts and ambiguous nested selections are excluded; prose-poetry paragraphs are preserved alongside verse.
- Associated-place review for `castellana-072`: [Universidad de Chile's Andrés Bello biography](https://uchile.cl/presentacion/historia/rectores-de-la-u-de-chile/andres-bello-lopez-1843-1865) documents his dates (1781–1865), Caracas origins, literary work during his London years (1810–1829), and career in Chile. Venezuela, England and Chile describe the poet's associations, not an asserted composition location; the date of the poem is left unestablished here.
- Associated-place review for `american-1922-00633`: the [Library of Virginia's Robert Nathaniel Dett biography](https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/dvb/bio/dett-r-nathaniel) documents his dates (1882–1943), Ontario birth and United States career. The [Academy of American Poets](https://poets.org/poem/rubinstein-staccato-etude) also credits the poem to him. Canada and the United States describe the poet's associations, not an asserted composition location; the date of the poem is left unestablished here.
- [*Livro de Mágoas*, Gutenberg 17610](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/17610): Florbela Espanca's 1919 collection, including the historical spelling of the transcription.
- Gonçalves Dias, *Cantos*, third edition (Leipzig: Brockhaus, 1860): four Brazilian Portuguese originals follow [Gutenberg 74854](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/74854), compared with the UFSC-hosted scan: [*Canção do Exilio*, pp. 3–4](https://literaturabrasileira.ufsc.br/midias/stream?id=117524&raw=1#page=31), [*Cachias*, pp. 11–12](https://literaturabrasileira.ufsc.br/midias/stream?id=117524&raw=1#page=39), [*Desejo*, p. 19](https://literaturabrasileira.ufsc.br/midias/stream?id=117524&raw=1#page=47), and [*Te Deum*, pp. 109–110](https://literaturabrasileira.ufsc.br/midias/stream?id=117524&raw=1#page=137). The texts retain the edition's spellings; the stanza in *Cachias* continues across a page break, rather than being divided there as in the Gutenberg transcription. The separately printed epigraphs are omitted from the poem text and acknowledged in the relevant reader contexts. The edition dates *Canção do Exilio* Coimbra, July 1843; other composition dates and places are not established here. The [Academia Brasileira de Letras biography](https://www.academia.org.br/academicos/goncalves-dias/biografia) documents the poet's Caxias, Maranhão birth and Brazilian career. *Te Deum* is a hymn of praise; its title recalls a [Christian hymn used in Catholic and Anglican traditions](https://www.encyclopedia.com/philosophy-and-religion/christianity/roman-catholic-and-orthodox-churches-liturgy-hymns-and-prayers/te-deum-laudamus), not evidence of the poet's denomination. No translations are supplied.
- [*Poems by Emily Dickinson*, Gutenberg 12242](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/12242): the first (1890) and second (1891) series, edited by Mabel Loomis Todd and T. W. Higginson. Their assigned titles and altered punctuation are not a diplomatic transcription of Dickinson's manuscripts. The poems are credited to the early published editions, not to modern editors.
- [*Les Fleurs du Mal*, Gutenberg 6099](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/6099): a limited set of separately headed French originals by Charles Baudelaire. The eBook does not identify its particular print edition and contains possible transcription errors; other headings that combine poems were not imported.
- Four English renderings of Baudelaire are separately attributed to F. P. Sturm and transcribed from [*Baudelaire: His Prose and Poetry*, edited by T. R. The contents of that edition identify Sturm as the translator; the translation rights note and source appear independently of each French original.

## Editorial standard

Keep author attribution, original text, edition, translator, dates, source, rights and historical evidence distinct. Use unknown or qualified values when facts are uncertain. Documented influence requires a citation; a shared theme or adjacent publication is not influence. Obtain permission before adding protected modern poems or translations. Never bulk-import texts merely to reach a target count.

## Discovery classification checklist

- Use neutral literary, language, time or geographic descriptions rather than categories based on sensitive personal identity. Do not infer sensitive traits from a name, language, anthology or poem.
- Make each category's label match its actual scope: a broad place category should include all matching recorded associations, while a single anthology should be identified as a selection. An associated place is not necessarily a nationality or the place of composition.
- Support new place associations with evidence recorded alongside the record or in this document; preserve uncertainty and use “Not established” when evidence is insufficient. An anthology's title or publication location alone does not establish every author's nationality. Existing anthology importers contain broad geographic defaults that require individual source review; do not treat those defaults as evidence for new classifications.
- Keep original texts, historical edition titles, citations and rights notes accurate, even if a historical title contains language unsuitable for a discovery label. Use a neutral display label without rewriting source records, existing filter values or URLs.
- Before publishing, check labels and counts against all matching works, including multiple place associations, and verify that source attribution and JavaScript-free reading remain intact.
